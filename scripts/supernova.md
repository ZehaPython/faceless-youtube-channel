## The common thread: THE WARNING

**Atlas is standing next to a star that's about to explode, and his only warning is the ghost particles that
escape first.** (Real science: in 1987, the neutrinos reached Earth hours before the light.)

## Voiceover-only text (for the voice tool)

> Because something just went straight through me. Neutrinos.
> A dying star throws out ninety-nine percent of its energy... as neutrinos.
> They escape first. In 1987, they hit Earth hours before the light.
> So why does a star explode? Because it fused itself... into iron.
> Stars burn by fusing atoms. But fusing iron eats energy instead.
> The fire dies. Gravity wins. The core collapses... and the star explodes.
> But here's the twist: you're made of this.
> The oxygen you breathe was made in stars like this... and blasted out when they exploded.
> Could one hurt us? Only closer than about thirty light-years.
> Betelgeuse is hundreds of light-years away. When it blows, it could shine like a full Moon. In daylight.
> Our galaxy gets one or two a century. The last one anyone saw here? 1604.
> There's the light. Time to go!

## Accuracy check (against `research/supernova.md`)

- "About to explode... maybe three hours" is story framing, based on fact 4 (the neutrinos arrived ~2–3 hours before the light in 1987). "Maybe" keeps it loose; the exact delay depends on the star.
- "Something just went straight through me. Neutrinos": neutrinos pass through almost everything (fact 3).
- "Ninety-nine percent of its energy... as neutrinos" matches fact 3 (core-collapse supernovae).
- "In 1987, they hit Earth hours before the light" matches fact 4 (SN 1987A, ~2–3 hours).
- "Fused itself into iron... fusing iron eats energy instead" matches fact 1.
- "The core collapses... and the star explodes" matches fact 2. "In a heartbeat" appears only in the shot description (visual), not the narration.
- "The oxygen you breathe was made in stars like this... and blasted out" matches fact 5 (massive stars make oxygen; core-collapse supernovae eject it).
- "Only closer than about thirty light-years" matches fact 10 (~30 ly; up to ~150 ly in special cases). The script uses the common figure; add the nuance in the description.
- "Betelgeuse is hundreds of light-years away" matches fact 8 (~500–700 ly).
- "Could shine like a full Moon. In daylight." matches fact 9 (estimates vary; "could" keeps it hedged).
- "Our galaxy gets one or two a century" is within fact 7 (estimates ~1–3 per century).
- "The last one anyone saw here? 1604" matches fact 7 (Kepler's Supernova, the last seen in the Milky Way).
- Not used: "outshines its galaxy" (fact 11, unverified) and gold (fact 6, off-thread).
