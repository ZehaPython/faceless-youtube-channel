## Voiceover-only text (for the voice tool)

> These rocks are making oxygen... in total darkness. Good. Because I'm running out.
> Four kilometers down, there's no sun. No plants. Nothing here should be making oxygen.
> Yet in 2024, scientists measured oxygen rising on this seafloor.
> The suspects? Metal lumps, grown over millions of years. Each carries about a volt. Most of an AA battery.
> Pack enough together and maybe... they split seawater. Into oxygen.
> So why isn't everyone celebrating? Mining companies want these rocks... for car batteries.
> And other scientists say the study is wrong.
> If it's real... mining them could take oxygen away from the life down here.
> And speaking of oxygen... I'm out.
> Made it. The watch says oxygen is far older than these rocks.
> Every atom in your lungs was forged... inside a star.
> And now it's pointing up. Pack a sweater. Actually... pack a helmet.

## Accuracy check (against the research brief)

- "These rocks are making oxygen" is the hook's mystery; the body hedges it ("scientists measured", "maybe", "the study is wrong").
- "Four kilometers down" matches fact 2. "Nothing here should be making oxygen": no light, no photosynthesis (fact 1). Deep water does hold oxygen carried down from the surface; the line is about *making* it.
- "In 2024, scientists measured oxygen rising" matches fact 1.
- "Grown over millions of years" matches fact 3. "About a volt... most of an AA battery": up to ~0.95 V vs 1.5 V (fact 4).
- "*Maybe* they split seawater" is hedged (fact 4).
- "Mining companies want these rocks for car batteries" matches fact 6. "Other scientists say the study is wrong" matches fact 7.
- "Mining them could take oxygen away from the life down here" is conditional. A study author warned against depleting "the oxygen source for deep-sea life" (Northwestern; fact 1 source).
- "Every atom [of oxygen] in your lungs was forged inside a star" matches fact 9. "Oxygen is far older than these rocks" is true (stellar oxygen predates Earth).
- Atlas's air running out is story, not a science claim.
