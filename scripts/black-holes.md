## Voiceover-only text (for the voice tool)

> When a giant star explodes, its core can collapse... into this. A black hole. And I'm drifting toward it.
> Is it really a hole? No. It's the opposite. A huge amount of stuff, crushed into a tiny space.
> Crush all of Earth that hard... and it would be the size of a marble.
> So why is it black? Because its gravity is so strong, not even light can get out.
> But there's an edge. The point of no return. It's called the event horizon.
> Outside it, a strong enough rocket can still escape. Inside, you'd have to go faster than light. Nothing can.
> Here's the twist: the edge isn't a surface. There's nothing to touch. Just a line you can never come back from.
> And if I get too close? Gravity pulls my feet much harder than my head. I'd be stretched... like spaghetti.
> Could one swallow Earth? No. The closest one we know is about sixteen hundred light-years away.
> But this one is right here. Thrusters... now!
> Still outside the edge. Still free.

## Accuracy check

- "A giant star explodes, its core can collapse into a black hole" matches supernova fact 20 / black-holes fact 13 ("can": only cores above ~2.8 Suns).
- "Huge amount of stuff crushed into a tiny space; Earth the size of a marble" matches fact 10 (radius ~8.9 mm, ~1.8 cm across).
- "Not even light can get out" matches facts 1 and 11.
- "Event horizon, point of no return; outside a rocket can escape, inside you'd need faster than light" matches fact 11 (NASA).
- "The edge isn't a surface" matches fact 11 (NASA: not a physical boundary).
- "Feet pulled harder than head, stretched like spaghetti" matches fact 2. Accurate for *this* black hole: a small, star-sized one stretches you before the edge. (Don't say it for giant ones, fact 3.)
- "Closest known one about 1,600 light-years away" matches fact 7 (Gaia BH1, ~1,560 ly). "Known" is important: closer ones may exist.
- Not used: time slowing down (fact 4), the 2022 photo (5), M87* (6), the singing black hole (8), TON 618 (9), the Sun-sized horizon (12). Saved for a later episode.
