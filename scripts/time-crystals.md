## Voiceover-only text (for the voice tool)

> This tiny crystal has a heartbeat... and physics said it was impossible.
> Normal crystals, like salt or diamond, repeat in space. Atom, atom, atom... over and over.
> In 2012, a Nobel-winning physicist asked: could something repeat in time instead?
> Other physicists ran the math. Their answer? No. Not on its own. That would be... perpetual motion.
> So scientists cheated. Just a little. They kicked atoms with lasers... tick, tick, tick.
> But the atoms didn't follow the beat. They flipped on every second kick.
> And they don't heat up and wear out like normal matter. In 2021, Google built one inside a quantum computer.
> A whole new phase of matter.
> Hang on... my watch just synced to its heartbeat. And now it's pointing... down.
> To the bottom of the ocean, where something is making oxygen... in total darkness.
> Pack a sweater.

## Accuracy check (against the research brief)

- "Physics said it was impossible" refers to the no-go result for systems at rest (fact 3). The script then explains the loophole, so it isn't misleading.
- "Flipped on every second kick" is period doubling (fact 4).
- "Don't heat up and wear out" follows the careful wording in the brief; it doesn't claim "free energy" or "forever".
- "In 2021, Google built one" matches fact 7. The journal citation still needs to go in the description before publishing.
- The outro's dark oxygen teaser is a real topic (next episode). Its facts get researched in that episode's brief.
