# time-crystals: opening shot (v1)

> **Superseded by `prompt-v2.md`.** v1 used the old lo-fi anime style and had no character.

Made by hand on the Higgsfield website (Kling 3.0, text-to-video). No research brief or script yet, so this is an
early style and hook test. The full pipeline (research, then script) still applies before the full Short.

## Settings
- Model: Kling 3.0, text-to-video
- Aspect ratio: 9:16
- Duration: 5 s
- Resolution: 1080p (720p for a cheaper test)
- Audio: off (voiceover and music are added in the editor)

## Prompt
Cozy lo-fi anime illustration, hand-painted 2D, Studio Ghibli inspired background art, clean dark-brown line art,
soft cel shading, subtle paper grain texture. Nighttime in a cluttered attic study. A single warm amber desk lamp
glows over a wooden desk covered in notebooks, pencils, a potted plant and a steaming mug. In the centre of the desk
sits a small glass dome. Inside it floats a tiny crystal made of glowing points of light arranged in a neat lattice.
The crystal pulses in a perfectly steady rhythm, like a slow heartbeat: its light points flip from soft teal to warm
amber and back, again and again, never fading. Beside it, an old brass pocket watch lies open, its second hand ticking.
Through the round window behind the desk, a deep blue night city with small yellow window lights. Deep blue-teal
shadows contrast with the warm lamp glow. Muted earthy palette of copper, forest green, brick red and cream. Calm,
curious, slightly magical mood. Slow, gentle camera push-in toward the glass dome. Steam drifts from the mug.
Vertical 9:16 composition, glass dome in the upper-middle of the frame.

## Negative prompt
text, letters, words, watermark, logo, subtitles, 3D render, photorealistic, glossy CGI, neon colours, harsh lighting,
people, faces, distorted hands, flickering, morphing objects, fast camera movement

## Hook text (added in the editor, not in the video)
"This Crystal Repeats in **Time**": 6 words, "Time" in amber #F2B66D.

## Science note for the script
A time crystal (more precisely, a discrete time crystal) is a group of particles whose arrangement repeats in time:
driven by a regular pulse, it flips back and forth at its own steady rhythm without heating up. The first ones were made
in 2016–2017 (Zhang et al., Choi et al.; Nature 2017), and Google showed one on a quantum processor in 2021
(Mi et al., Nature 2022). The research brief must verify these sources before scripting.
