# Research brief: black-holes (Episode 4)

_Prepared 2026-10-27. Competitor check: **40 titles** mention black holes across Kurzgesagt + 8 channels; Kurzgesagt
alone has 6 videos at 19–32M views ("What If You Fall into a Black Hole?", "Black Holes Explained", "The Largest
Black Hole..."). Huge demand, crowded topic. **We need angles they haven't used as a title**: the nearest black hole,
the black hole that "sings", and the falling-in experience told through Atlas's own clock._

## One-line idea

A black hole is gravity so strong that not even light escapes. Fall into a small one and you're stretched like
spaghetti; fall into a giant one and you might not even notice crossing the edge. And time itself slows down.

## Key facts

| 2 | **Spaghettification:** near a black hole, gravity pulls much harder on your feet than your head, stretching you. ## Suggested common thread and Atlas's danger

- **Thread:** *"Atlas is falling toward a black hole, and his own pocket watch shows time slowing down."*
  (The watch returns only as a normal prop, no series device.) Time is both the topic and the danger.
- **Tracker on screen:** two clocks, Atlas's and a beacon left behind, drifting further apart.
- **Twist (~60%):** "The *small* black hole is the dangerous one. The giant one, you'd barely feel crossing." (facts 2–3)
- **Your question answered:** "Is there one near us? The closest we know is 1,560 light-years away. Safe." (7)
- **Resolution last:** he fires his thrusters and escapes, older or younger than the outside world.

## Wow-fact candidates (pick 5–7)
- You could cross a giant black hole's edge without noticing (3)
- Small black holes are the deadly ones (2)
- Outsiders would see you freeze at the edge forever (4)
- The closest black hole is "only" 1,560 light-years away and was found in 2022 (7)
- A black hole sings a B-flat, 57 octaves too low to hear (8)
- The first photo of our galaxy's black hole is from 2022 (5)
- The biggest weighs tens of billions of Suns (9)

## Careful wording
- Time dilation: "time runs slower *compared with* far-away observers", not "time stops".
- "You'd barely feel crossing" only for **supermassive** black holes, and add that it gets worse inside.
- TON 618: "tens of billions of Suns" (estimates vary from ~40 to ~66 billion).
- Perseus "sound": it's pressure waves in gas, not sound travelling through empty space.
