# Research brief: supernova (Episode 3)

_Prepared 2026-10-27. Competitor check: only 3 titles mention supernovae across Kurzgesagt + 8 channels
(~9,100 titles; none from Kurzgesagt). Strong search demand, little Shorts competition._

## One-line idea

When a giant star runs out of fuel, its core collapses in moments and the star explodes. That explosion spread
the oxygen, calcium and iron that you are made of. One of the most famous stars in our sky, Betelgeuse, will do it.

## Key facts

| # | Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stars shine by fusing light elements into heavier ones. Fusion stops at **iron**: fusing iron *takes* energy instead of releasing it, so the core loses its power source. | Verified (textbook; forum-level sources) | [Universe Today: Type II supernovae](https://www.universetoday.com/articles/astronomy-jargon-101-type-ii-supernovae), [Physics Forums](https://www.physicsforums.com/threads/does-iron-really-kill-fusion-in-stars.596972/post-3867562) |
| 2 | Without fusion pushing out, gravity wins: the iron core collapses, electrons are squeezed into protons, and the core becomes a ball of neutrons (a newborn neutron star). The star's outer layers are blasted away. | Verified | [Universe Today](https://www.universetoday.com/articles/astronomy-jargon-101-type-ii-supernovae), [Physics Forums](https://www.physicsforums.com/threads/why-does-the-core-collapse-happen-so-fast-in-a-supernova.1047601/post-6825490) |
| 3 | About **99% of the explosion's energy escapes as neutrinos**, ghostly particles that pass through almost everything. | Verified (review paper) | [arXiv 1605.02204](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.02204) |
| 4 | **SN 1987A** (in the Large Magellanic Cloud): about two dozen neutrinos were caught by detectors on Earth, arriving **~2–3 hours before the light**. | Verified | [Wikipedia: SN 1987A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SN_1987A), [arXiv 2509.16306](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16306v1) |
| 5 | Massive stars build oxygen in their inner layers; core-collapse supernovae throw it out into space, where it ends up in new stars, planets and people. | Verified (research papers) | [arXiv 1712.02013](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.02013), [arXiv astro-ph/0010021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/astro-ph/0010021) |
| 6 | Gold: supernovae make some, but colliding neutron stars likely make much more ("perhaps a moon's worth" vs. "a Jupiter-mass"); the full origin of gold is still debated. | Verified (debated) | [Quanta Magazine](https://www.quantamagazine.org/did-neutron-stars-or-supernovas-forge-the-universes-supply-of-gold-20170323/), [Space.com](https://space.com/where-did-gold-come-from.html), [IFLScience](https://www.iflscience.com/space/neutron-star-collisions-arent-creating-enough-gold-so-where-is-it-coming-from/) |
| 7 | The Milky Way has roughly **1–3 supernovae per century** (estimates vary), but dust hides most of them. The last one *seen* in our galaxy was **Kepler's Supernova in 1604**. | Verified | [Wikipedia: Supernova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supernova), [Universe Today](https://www.universetoday.com/articles/there-should-be-a-few-supernovae-in-the-milky-way-every-century-but-weve-only-seen-5-in-the-last-1000-years-why), [Panagia (NED)](https://ned.ipac.caltech.edu/level5/March01/Panagia/Panagia1.html) |
| 8 | **Betelgeuse** (Orion's shoulder) is ~500–700 light-years away (estimates vary) and expected to explode sometime within ~100,000 years. | Verified | [BBC Sky at Night](https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/space-science/earth-danger-betelgeuse-supernova/), [BGR (2020 distance study)](https://bgr.com/2020/10/16/betelgeuse-distance-star-supernova-size/) |
| 9 | When Betelgeuse explodes, it could shine roughly **as bright as the full Moon**, be **visible in daylight** and cast sharp shadows at night for weeks, then fade over months. | Verified (secondary; brightness estimates vary) | [BBC Sky at Night](https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/space-science/earth-danger-betelgeuse-supernova/), [Urban Astronomer](https://www.urban-astronomer.com/astronomy/nearby-supernova/) |

## Suggested common thread and Atlas's danger

- **Thread:** *"Atlas is standing on a planet next to a star that's about to explode, and the only warning is the
  ghostly neutrinos that arrive first."* The neutrinos-before-light fact (4) is a real built-in countdown.
- **Tracker on screen:** a brass **neutrino detector** dial that starts clicking faster (or a countdown between the
  neutrino alarm and the light arriving).
- **Danger:** the shockwave and light. Resolution last: he escapes just as the light hits.
- **Viewer stakes / "your question answered":** "Will Betelgeuse hurt us? No, it's too far. But you'll see it in
  daylight." (facts 9–10)
- **Biggest wow fact for the end:** the oxygen in your lungs and the calcium in your bones were blasted out by
  explosions like this (facts 5, plus Ep 2's "two-thirds oxygen").

## Wow-fact candidates (pick 5–7)
- Fusion stops at iron; iron kills the star (1)
- 99% of the energy is invisible neutrinos (3)
- In 1987, neutrinos reached Earth hours *before* the light (4)
- Last supernova seen in our galaxy: 1604, before telescopes were used for astronomy (7)
- Betelgeuse could be visible in daylight, as bright as the full Moon (9)
- Your oxygen came from exploded stars (5)
- Gold mostly comes from colliding dead stars, not supernovae (6), a good "twist" candidate

## Careful wording
- Betelgeuse: "sometime in the next ~100,000 years", never "soon" or "any day now".
- Brightness: "could be as bright as the full Moon" (estimates vary).
- Gold: "colliding neutron stars likely make most of it", since it's still debated.
- Don't say "outshines the galaxy" until fact 11 is verified.
