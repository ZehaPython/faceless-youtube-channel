# Research brief: dark-forest (Episode 5)

_Prepared 2026-10-27. The creator asked for "the Black Forest theorem"; this is the **Dark Forest theory**
(confirm with the creator). Competitor check: Kurzgesagt covered it ("Why We Should NOT Look For Aliens – The Dark
Forest", ~17M views) plus two Fermi-paradox videos (17–35M). Proven demand. **Our angle must be different**: the
messages humanity has *already* sent (Arecibo 1974), and the one signal we may have heard (Wow!, 1977)._

## One-line idea

If the universe is so big, where is everybody? One chilling answer, the Dark Forest: every civilisation is a silent
hunter hiding in a dark forest, and anyone who reveals themselves gets destroyed. And in 1974, we shouted.

**Important:** the Dark Forest is a **science-fiction idea / thought experiment**, popularised by Liu Cixin's novel
*The Dark Forest* (2008, sequel to *The Three-Body Problem*). It is not a scientific theory with evidence. The
script must say so clearly.

## Suggested common thread and Atlas's danger

- **Thread:** *"Atlas picks up an alien signal and has to decide: answer it... or stay silent."*
  The topic (whether to reveal ourselves) and Atlas's dilemma are the same thing.
- **Tracker on screen:** his brass radio's "transmit" lever and a blinking reply light, or the signal's strength.
- **Twist (~60%):** "But here's the thing: we already answered. In 1974." (Arecibo, fact 5)
- **Your question answered:** "Is anyone coming? That message won't even arrive for 25,000 years." (6)
- **Honesty beat:** "It's an idea from a science-fiction novel, not a proven fact. But scientists still argue about it." (2, 4, 7)
- **Resolution last:** Atlas switches off his transmitter, the forest goes quiet, and something blinks in the dark.

## Careful wording
- Always frame it as **an idea / a science-fiction thought experiment**, never "scientists proved" or "this is why
  we haven't found aliens".
- Wow! signal: "never explained for sure; recent studies point to a natural source", not "an alien message".
- Arecibo distance: "about 25,000 light-years".
- Credit the novel by name (Liu Cixin, *The Dark Forest*).
